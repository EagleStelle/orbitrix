# Maps and Map Editor

Open **Maps** from Home or from the Board preview in Setup. Choose a saved map, or choose **Classic board** to use the existing rectangular presets. Maps are saved on this device, never uploaded to a catalogue. Browser storage belongs to the current browser and site; export files for backups.

The library starts with Crossroads, Long Distance, and Pressure Points. Select a map to edit, rename, duplicate, delete, export, share, or use it. Invalid designs remain editable drafts; they cannot start a match.

## Terms and tools

A **map** is the saved design. A **board** is that design in a match. A **cell** has base **terrain** and optionally a passive **feature** (Link, Portal, Reservoir, Spillway, Furnace, Crusher, Vault, Turret, Box or Spring) or a **circuit component**. An **edge** is the boundary between adjacent cells; a **connection** is a route an explosion actually uses. **Capacity** is the orb count that triggers an explosion.

| Element | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Floor | Playable cell. Capacity equals its open connections. |
| Wall | Permanent obstruction occupying a cell. |
| Void | Missing surface, outside the playable area. Blocks connections like Wall. |
| Hole | Unplayable, but counts toward neighboring Floor capacity; orbs sent into it are lost. Not a Portal exit. |
| Landfill | Unplayable and counts as a route without lowering neighboring capacity. Absorbs incoming orbs as neutral fill up to the capacity it would have as Floor in the starting layout (two to four), then becomes empty, unowned Floor. Several may arrive in one wave; placement is illegal until complete. Portals can feed it. |
| Breakable Wall | Starts with two durability. Each adjacent exploding cell deals one damage per wave; destruction creates empty Floor. |
| Breakable Floor | Playable Floor with two durability. Each of its own explosions deals one damage; neighbors' explosions don't. Destruction creates Void for following waves, Breakable Wall's counterpart: the orbs left on it, including any that arrived in that wave, are lost, and each neighbor loses that route, as when a Door closes. Features and circuit components need plain Floor. |
| Sinkhole | Playable Floor that never explodes: once its orbs reach its capacity it collapses into a Hole, and they fall in and are lost. Neighbors keep their capacity, since a Hole still counts as a route. Landfill's counterpart. Features and circuit components need plain Floor. |
| Link | A pair of distant Floor cells are extra neighbors. Each endpoint sends one orb to the other when exploding. Arrivals land normally. |
| Portal | An arriving orb passes through its pair and continues in the same direction to the next Floor or Landfill cell on an enabled axis. No placement or orb storage. Enabled routes replace cardinal connections; disabled axes send no orbs and do not count toward nearby capacity. |
| Reservoir | Capacity is one above the number of connections. Explosions send one orb per connection and the extra orb along its arrow, so that side gets two; then a new orb appears in it, keeping it with the exploding player. The arrow then turns clockwise to the next open route. It makes one orb a move, on its first explosion. |
| Spillway | Capacity is one above the number of connections. Explosions send one orb per connection; the extra orb is lost, as in a Hole. |
| Furnace | Normal capacity. After each move by the player whose orbs are on it, it gains one orb; at capacity it explodes as usual. Neutral when empty. It skips the first of those moves after changing hands or after a move leaves it empty; one that pops and is refilled by the same player within the move keeps working. |
| Crusher | Normal capacity. After each move by the player whose orbs are on it, it loses one orb until empty, then turns neutral. It skips the first of those moves after changing hands or after a move leaves it empty; one that pops and is refilled by the same player within the move keeps working. |
| Vault | Normal capacity. After each move by the player whose orbs are on it, it raises a two-hit shield if it has none; a damaged shield stays as it is. Each other player's orb arriving breaks one hit and is lost; the holder's own orbs land normally. Once broken it can be captured, and the holder's next move raises a new one. It skips the first of those moves after changing hands or after a move leaves it empty, which also drops its shield; one that pops and is refilled by the same player within the move keeps working, and its shield guards it while empty. A shield stops Boxes like a Wall; a stack a Box shoves into it loses one orb per hit left, and the rest capture it. |
| Turret | Normal capacity. After each move by the player whose orbs are on it, it fires one new orb into the cell it faces, which lands and captures like any orb, then turns clockwise to its next open Floor route: up, right, down, left. After four shots it rearms, skipping that player's next move, then fires four more. It skips the first move after changing hands or after a move leaves it empty, which restarts its four shots but never its facing. It fires only at Floor, never into a Hole or Landfill, and not at all when shut in. |
| Box | A crate that starts on Floor, holds no orbs and takes no placement, but counts as a route. Orbs exploded into it push it one cell; pushes in one wave add up, opposite ones cancelling and crossing ones moving it diagonally. The pushing orbs land where it stood. Orbs in its way slide one cell further, merging into that cell (whose owner they capture), dropping into a Hole or filling a Landfill, and popping in the next wave if over capacity. Blocked straight ahead (Wall, Void, the board edge, a closed Door, a Barrier or closed Gate, another Box), it bounces back the other way instead. It stays, sending the orbs back, when pushes cancel, the orbs ahead can't move, a diagonal is blocked or two moves want one cell. It passes over any Floor, presses switches (Hold while it stands there), rides Portals when moving straight, and fills the first Hole or Landfill it enters, which becomes Floor: only Boxes fill Holes. |
| Spring | Stands on Floor against exactly one Wall or Barrier and faces away from it. Holds no orbs and takes no placement, but counts as a route: an orb or Box arriving from a side leaves into the cell in front; one arriving from the front goes straight back (a Box bounces off it like a Wall). |
| Latch | Reverses its circuit once on accepted placement or arrival. Another switch's transition rearms it for a later arrival; exploding by itself never presses it. |
| Toggle | Flips when an orb is placed into its empty cell, or once per wave when orbs pop into or out of it. Adding orbs without a pop does not flip it. |
| Hold | Pressed while occupied; pressing and releasing each reverse its circuit. |
| Barrier | Permanently blocks the connection across an edge, including routes into a Landfill. |
| Breakable Barrier | Blocks an edge between Floor or Landfill cells, starting with two durability. Only those two cells damage it when exploding, one damage each per wave. Destruction opens the connection for following waves. |
| Gate | An edge obstruction. Each circuit reversal swaps its configured open/closed starting state. |
| Door | A whole-cell wall when closed, playable Floor when open. Each circuit reversal swaps its configured starting state. Orbs caught inside a closing Door remain owned and dormant until it reopens. |

The palette groups **Terrain** (Void, Hole, Landfill, Sinkhole, Floor, Wall, Barrier, Breakable Floor, Breakable Wall, Breakable Barrier), **Devices** (Link, Portal, Reservoir, Spillway, Furnace, Crusher, Vault, Turret, Box, Spring), and **Circuit** (Latch, Toggle, Hold, Gate, Door). Desktop shows an icon-and-label list; short landscape uses a category picker and portrait uses tabs with a scrolling shelf. The inspector shows functional help and a color swatch. For Gate and Door, tap **Starts closed** or **Starts open** before painting. Repaint an existing component to change its starting state; Undo/Redo and Save preserve it. Each output has its own starting state, so one circuit can contain both initially open and initially closed outputs. The same visible control works with touch and mouse on every target.

Edit the map name in the header, beside **Test**, **Save**, and **More** (the three-dot button). Undo, Redo, Erase, Pan, zoom out, Fit, and zoom in remain beside the canvas. Drag to paint one undoable stroke. Edge tools trace the drag between grid corners: horizontal and vertical passes stay on that line, and a held drag can turn corners without wrapping each Floor cell. A tap places one edge. Erase removes an edge obstruction when it hits one; otherwise it restores Floor. Pan and zoom make large maps reachable; Fit resets both.

Link and Portal placement each take two distant Floor endpoints: choose an unused **Pair color**, then paint the two endpoints. The inspector's **Pair color** or **Circuit color** swatch opens a single page of twelve group colors, taken directly from the orb palette in the same order. Link pairs, Portal pairs and switch circuits use separate identity sets within those twelve colors; the same color may identify one of each. Map art and relationship swatches stay identical with Colorblind enabled or disabled. Neither state adds black/white stamps, notches, or identity patterns; player and orb marks elsewhere stay unchanged. A contextual line helps find a Link's or Portal's partner during placement or hover.

With the Portal tool selected, choose **All directions**, **Horizontal**, or **Vertical** before placing a pair. Tap an existing Portal to inspect its pair; changing the direction setting updates both endpoints in one undo step. If physical obstructions leave only one available axis, the control shows that axis with a lock and is disabled. If neither axis is open, it reads **No open route** and the draft is unplayable.

The toolbar has capacity and reversed-circuit preview toggles. Preview reverses every circuit relative to its outputs' saved starting states and shows switches pressed; capacities follow that preview. It does not change the saved design. **More** contains map dimensions. Validation messages appear in the bottom snackbar; affected cells and edges are outlined. Invalid drafts can be saved, but Test remains disabled until they are valid.

Save commits the draft to the library. Unsaved work is recovered separately after editing stops; **Recover draft** resumes it. Leaving an edited map asks before discarding changes. **Test** opens the familiar player/bot setup with private preferences. Escape/Android Back returns from test play to the unchanged editor draft.

Saving a playable revision of the selected map also updates the selection for normal play. On startup, a saved selection refreshes from its uniquely named library entry; missing, ambiguous or unplayable entries leave the prior playable selection intact. Ongoing matches retain their private map copy.

## Valid maps

Dimensions are 3–40 cells per side. With outputs in their configured starting states, Links and Portal routes included, each initially playable Floor must have capacity of at least two. Portal endpoints do not count as playable Floor for capacity checks or player limits. A Reservoir or Spillway makes Floor with one open connection valid by raising its capacity to two; Floor with no open connections remains invalid even with one. Every Breakable Wall must border at least two initial Floor cells. Furnaces and Crushers need capacity of at least three. These checks preserve the opening round. The strip under the editor canvas estimates the average moves of a game and places it from Instant to Endless against an empty board of the same room. Holes slow games most, since every open Floor side loses an orb, then Crushers, then Spillways and Vaults (whose shields destroy orbs); Furnaces speed them up, each a little less as they crowd and less still beside a Hole; more players make Furnace maps run longer. Reservoirs speed them up a little, Turrets barely. The count matters, not the board size. Fast and Slow are fine; Instant (Furnaces or Reservoirs ending games within about ten moves, which only tiny boards reach) and Endless (over two and a half times, where many games never end) are issues that mark the Furnaces, Reservoirs and Turrets, or the Holes, Sinkholes, Spillways, Crushers and Vaults. Breakable Floors leave the pace alone but shorten the estimate a little: each takes its room and a route from each neighbor when it breaks. Landfills leave the pace alone but lengthen the estimate: each orb they absorb costs about a move before their Floor adds room. Sinkholes fill and collapse early, so they count as the Holes they become. Boxes fill Holes, so Holes with a Box for each drain only until filled; the estimate assumes every Box reaches one. Boxes and Springs otherwise leave the pace alone, and a Spring takes its own cell's room.

Landfills are excluded from initially playable Floor and player limits. Each needs a fill target of at least two, the capacity it would have as Floor, so the Floor it becomes can't explode on its first orb. Breakable Floors count as initially playable Floor for capacity, Breakable Wall neighbors, edges and player limits. Validation also assumes every Breakable Floor broken: each cell that is or can become Floor must keep at least one route, or its orbs could never explode or be captured, and the rest of the Floor must still connect without them. Sinkholes also count as initially playable Floor, and the rest of the Floor must connect without them too: players split apart once they collapse could never meet. A collapsed Sinkhole still counts as a route, so it seals no cell.

All playable Floor, including closed Doors, must eventually connect, counting Landfills and Breakable Walls as future Floor and Breakable Barriers as future open connections. Every Landfill must belong to that reachable future Floor; unreachable Landfills are invalid. Gates may separate Floor groups when their matching switch is reachable from at least one side without opening that Gate first. Reachable Gates and Doors can connect further switches and outputs; circular dependencies are invalid. Links and Portal routes also connect Floor groups. Wall, Void, and permanent Barriers remain permanent obstructions, so regions enclosed only by them are invalid.

A Spring needs exactly one orthogonal Wall or Barrier behind it (Breakable Walls and Barriers, Gates, Void and the board edge don't count) and, in front with no edge between, Floor without a Portal, Spring or Door, a Hole or a Landfill. A Box starts on plain Floor, which must meet the Floor rules since the Box moves on; Box and Spring cells don't count toward the player limit. A Portal side facing a Spring is blocked, as one facing a Hole is.

Link and Portal pairs each need exactly two distinct, nonadjacent Floor endpoints. Features and circuit components require Floor and cannot overlap. Both kinds of Barrier and Gates require orthogonally adjacent Floor or Landfill cells, or either beside a Hole, with only one element on that edge. A Hole needs a Floor side not sealed by a Barrier; Gates don't seal. Barriers and closed Gates prevent a Landfill from receiving across that edge; destroying a breakable edge opens that route for following waves. Each circuit needs at least one switch and at least one Gate or Door. Any mixture and number of switches may share a circuit, within the map's cell limit. Player count cannot exceed initially playable Floor count; closed Doors are excluded.

A blocked side at either Portal endpoint disables that entire horizontal or vertical axis across both endpoints. Wall, Void, the board edge, a closed Door, or a blocked edge can disable an axis. A pair must have at least one open axis in its starting layout; one blocked axis restricts travel to the other. Enabled routes may pass through further Portal pairs and count as connections only when they reach playable Floor or an unfilled Landfill; a permanent Hole remains an invalid Portal exit. Nearby Floor must still meet the capacity and connectivity rules above.

The saved direction setting is retained when an obstruction forces a fallback to the other available axis. Destroying a Breakable Wall or Breakable Barrier, or changing a Gate or Door, recalculates the available axes and can restore the requested directions. Disabled axes send no orbs and contribute no connection to neighboring capacity.

Playable maps use group identities 1–12. Older maps with higher group identities remain saveable, shareable drafts, with the affected elements highlighted; reassign those elements to one of the twelve colors before starting a match. Imports do not silently recolor or discard them.

## Shared map art

Palette, canvas, library thumbnail, and match board share the same component art and the map-device rules in DESIGN.md. Devices use one outline weight, `max(0.8 px, cell size * 0.04)`, Wall-colored (`sc.dim`) neutral strokes, no circles, and no incidental detail. Solid fills differ from Floor and Void. All device outer frames share exact Floor tile bounds, `r.inset(cell * tileGutter)`, and radius `cell * tileRadius`, with no surrounding Floor gap. All three switches share inner inset `body.w * 0.1875` and inner radius `max(0, outerRadius - inset)`; Latch and Hold have identical inner rectangles.

Portals reuse Link's exact pair ink, rounded-square frame and dark opening, with four angular doorway brackets for All directions and a clear central passage. Horizontal keeps the left and right brackets; Vertical keeps the top and bottom brackets. Disabled axes lose their corresponding brackets at both endpoints. The frame keeps the exact Floor footprint and corner radius; the brackets use the shared device stroke, with no petal shapes. Palette, editor, thumbnails and match use the same art in both themes and Colorblind states.

Links have a rounded-square colored rim and dark opening using the exact Floor corner radius, with two separated U paths reproducing the large lower arc and small upper arc. Their internal bends inherit the outer rounded geometry with inset-derived radius `min(halfSpan, max(strokeWidth, frameRadius - (body.w / 2 - halfSpan)))`, at least the stroke width within the path span; no right-angle bends. Arrangement, tips, width and pair ink stay unchanged. The opening uses `mix(ink, dim, 0.18)` in light mode and `mix(ground, dim, 0.18)` in dark mode. The two paths always remain visible with Colorblind enabled or disabled. Latch retains its rounded-square housing and button with normalized strokes, outlined OFF/released and solid circuit-color fill ON/pressed with unchanged geometry. Toggle uses the same rounded outer frame and full shared inner rectangle and gaps as the other switches. Its button has two equal adjacent halves and a center seam: left solid circuit-color fill means OFF, right solid fill means ON, with the opposite half outlined. The selected-half fill is clipped to the rounded inner geometry; the full inner outline and vertical seam use the shared stroke width. Hold is top-down: four corner brackets clipped from the shared rounded frame surround a central plate identical to Latch's inner rectangle, outlined when released and filled in circuit color when pressed. Reservoir is a top-down square well with the exact Floor radius on its outer outline and one unchanged square inset reserve pocket representing the one orb retained after exploding, without a machine housing or side view. Both Reservoir outlines are neutral Wall ink when empty, use the occupying player's exact ink while occupied, and return to neutral when empty. Ownership follows the displayed animation wave. Four symmetrical cardinal channel strokes extend from the central square pocket toward the outer basin, stopping two stroke widths before the frame, with the same shared stroke and neutral/owner ink. The channel its extra orb leaves by ends in an open arrowhead: two strokes back at 45°, 13% of the body each, in the same stroke and ink, never filled. It points at the first open route clockwise from the arrow's step, turns after each explosion with the displayed wave, and turns with the board in landscape; palette icons point up. Frame and radius stay unchanged. A Spillway uses the same frame, stroke and neutral/owner ink logic, but four diagonal channels run from its frame corners into a square pocket filled with the Holes' pit ink; its extra orb drops into that pocket and fades like one sent into a Hole. Furnace and Crusher share that frame, stroke and neutral/owner ink without a pocket: a Furnace has eight rays bursting from an open centre, a Crusher two arrows pressing onto a flat line. Both sit inside Hold's broken frame until they have worked for their holder, and the full frame after. A crushed orb shrinks and fades at the centre like one sent into a Hole. A Vault shares that frame and ink with a hexagon outline. Its raised shield is drawn over the orbs in the holder's ink: a veil tinted toward that ink washes the orbs as if behind glass, under a glow that rises toward the tile's edge, the one gradient in the map art. A shield with one hit left looks the same, cracked with Breakable Floor's fracture at its damaged size in the other theme's mat colour. An orb the shield takes stops at the tile's edge and shrinks and fades there like one sent into a Hole. A Turret shares that frame and ink, seen from above: a square base and one filled barrel with a wider muzzle, joined to the base and pointing where it fires next; it turns with the displayed wave and with the board in landscape. Map art uses no circles. Links and switches retain their pair/circuit inks.

Devices, terrain, Gates and relationship swatches keep identical normal art in both Colorblind states, with no map group marks or alternate symbols. Player and orb symbols elsewhere are unchanged. Wall fill and joins and structural Gate/Barrier thickness remain unchanged.

Breakable walls show a connected, tapered negative-space fracture rooted at the center: three main splits and two forks, visibly identifying breakability at initial durability 2. At durability 1 the same whole connected network grows 1.24x, longer and wider toward the edges. Use branching fractures rather than repetitive zigzags; no random texture, detached flecks or circles. Fracture cuts are structural damage, not additional device-icon stroke weights. Wall fill and joins stay unchanged; destruction creates empty Floor.

Breakable Floor is a Floor tile shifted the opposite way from Breakable Wall's ink (darker in the light theme, lighter in the dark one), with the same fracture in the other theme's mat colour, the reverse of Breakable Wall's crack (dark in the light theme, light in the dark one), growing after one hit in the same way. Breaking leaves Void, and its orbs drop away like ones sent into a Hole, with the breakable wall's destruction cue.

A Sinkhole shares Breakable Floor's fill, with a ragged dark hole torn through its centre in the Holes' Pit ink. When it collapses it becomes the normal Hole art, joining neighbouring Holes, and its orbs fall in like ones sent into a Hole, with the Hole's falling cue.

A Box is a crate filling the Floor tile, a step from Floor toward Wall ink, with the device outline, an inner frame and an X brace in Wall ink. Where a Box fills a Hole or Landfill, the new Floor shows that same crate, smaller, set into it, on Floor's own fill with darker lines. A Spring is seen side on: a looped coil as wide as the tile and a pad plate, in the device stroke and Wall ink, standing on a Barrier-thick mount on the edge to its Wall (a Barrier behind it is its own mount) and reaching about 80% across the tile, so its open front shows where it launches; it turns with the board in landscape. The mount is as wide as the Wall, which reaches it with no gap, and runs on to meet a Barrier, Gate or neighbouring mount at the corner; its coil is the one curved device art. In a match Boxes slide at the pace of the wave's flying orbs, the orbs they shove sliding beside them; orbs that hit a Box that stays fly half way and back, and a Box that fills a Hole or Landfill falls in like the orb that completes a Landfill: it shrinks and turns into the crate the Floor will show, and the pit turns to Floor around it once it has landed, holding playback until then. A Spring squashes as it launches.

Breakable Barriers retain the permanent Barrier's thickness and use Breakable Wall's slightly shifted ink, with a transverse negative-space fracture that widens after damage. Destruction removes the edge art and its wall joins. The same art appears in the palette, editor, thumbnails and displayed match wave, in both themes and Colorblind states.

Holes and Landfills share one pit art, seen straight from above: flat bands inside the Floor footprint, each smaller and darker than the one before, never lighter. A Hole's bands step down to a black core, an endless pit; orbs sent into it drop from its centre and fade. A Landfill uses the same bands but stops one earlier and lighter, and its last band is the bottom that catches orbs. Exact band sizes and inks are in DESIGN.md. Adjacent Holes join each other, and adjacent unfilled Landfills join each other, in straight, L and block shapes; each band draws across the whole connected opening before the next. Completed Landfills stop joining and show Floor. The same art appears in the palette, editor, library thumbnails and match, in both themes and Colorblind states.

Incoming Landfill orbs shrink as they fall onto the bottom, turning neutral and losing Colorblind marks. They rest as a centred clump inside the bottom's darkest square, overlapping like orbs on Floor (a lone orb sits in the middle), sliding together into a bigger clump as each one falls in, and otherwise keep still; Floor appears once the completing orb has landed on the bottom, with its own rising fill cue. Playback stays active until it settles, holding following waves and the next turn; fast-forward bypasses the wait. Portal-fed arrivals retain their flight path and chime.

## Sharing and files

Exports use `.orbitrixmap`, portable format version 2, with zlib compression, a 64 KiB bound on both file and decompressed payload, and a checksum. Share codes start with `orbitrix-map2.`. Files, share codes, and QR payloads carry the same map definition. Files and pasted codes work offline. Imports show a preview and require **Save**; they never replace another map automatically.

Earlier format-1 and format-2 files/codes still import. Their Switch, ToggleSwitch and HoldSwitch tags become Latch, Toggle and Hold circuit components; unconfigured outputs start closed. Multiple controllers are preserved and follow the new circuit rules. Door and initial open states use previously unused format-2 tags/bits; Portal uses component tags 7 (All directions), 8 (Horizontal), and 9 (Vertical), with its pair group in the existing group byte. `PortalMode` defaults to zero (All directions), so earlier tag-7 Portals retain that setting on import. Save, file export, share codes and QR preserve both endpoints, their group and requested direction setting. Temporary format-3 exports from the earlier circuit build also import and re-export as format 2. Import never removes components or overwrites stored designs. Older development builds cannot read the new Door/state or Portal extensions; keep backups when using those builds.

In browsers and native builds configured with `ORBITRIX_SERVER`, QR sharing uses `/play#map=…`. The complete map is in the URL fragment and is not sent to the website. The link targets the game's import preview; the phone-camera handoff still needs device verification. Loading the web application may require connectivity. Large payloads use file/code sharing instead of an impractically dense QR. Native development builds without a configured website provide code/file sharing; their raw code QR requires a scanner capable of copying text.

Browser imports use a file picker and exports download a file. Android uses the system document picker. Other native builds export into the app data directory's `exports` folder and accept a pasted file path for import.

In a room the host sends the selected map automatically. Joining does not add it to your library. Use **Save map** in the match menu to preview and save a copy.

## Rules and compatibility

Explosions preserve every orb except those sent into a Hole or unfilled Landfill (a Box's shove included), a Spillway's extra orb, those on a Breakable Floor as it breaks, those on a Sinkhole as it collapses and those a Vault's shield takes, and add the orb each exploding Reservoir makes once a move and the one each Turret fires: each accepted placement adds exactly one, and no other terrain creates or consumes them. Once a move's chain settles, every Furnace the mover holds gains one orb and every Crusher they hold loses one, every Vault they hold without a shield raises one and every Turret they hold fires, except the first time after either changes hands or a move leaves it empty (one refilled by its holder within the move keeps working); Furnaces that reach capacity then explode as a further chain, shown at once, and the Turrets' shots fly in its first wave. A host's Skip runs neither. A direct accepted placement updates its switch and circuit immediately, before choosing the first exploding cells. This is recorded as terrain events at wave −1.

Each explosion wave uses its starting connections. Transfers finish before wall damage and circuit inputs apply together. Toggle fires on empty-cell placement, or once when receiving or exploding in a wave, including when both occur. Hold changes on occupancy transitions. Latch fires once until another switch transition rearms it; rearming itself does not reverse the circuit. Inputs reverse circuit polarity, rather than assigning an open state: two simultaneous inputs cancel, three reverse once. Switch states remain individual even when inputs cancel. A circuit's polarity and each output's starting state determine its open/closed state. Connections and capacities rebuild before selecting the next wave. Victory is checked after the current wave's effects.

A Breakable Floor breaks with the wall damage after the transfers of its second explosion's wave: it still sends its orbs out, and those left over or arriving in that wave fall away. Its neighbors lose that route and their capacity drops, so any now at capacity explode in the next wave. Losing every orb this way eliminates a player like any other loss.

Boxes move after a wave's transfers, with its wall damage and circuit inputs, against where Boxes and terrain stood when the wave began, so the result never depends on order; a Box can't enter a cell another Box leaves in the same wave. Orbs a Box shoves pop in the next wave. A Vault's shield stops a Box as it stands after the wave's transfers. A Box that bounces back onto a breaking Breakable Floor falls with it; one shut in by a closing Door waits there until it opens.

A Sinkhole collapses where a Floor would explode. A placement that fills it collapses it before the first exploding cells are chosen, recorded at wave −1 like a switch press, so nothing explodes. Arrivals that fill it, or a Gate, Door or broken floor that drops its capacity under its orbs, collapse it with the wall damage after that wave's transfers, never as part of the next wave. Its orbs, the last arrivals included, fall in and are lost. Its neighbors keep their capacity; a Portal route ending in it closes that axis, as for any Hole.

Filling a Landfill uses the same starting topology: all arrivals in its completing wave are consumed, even if more arrive than the remaining fill needs. It becomes empty, unowned Floor for the next wave, never partway through the completing wave. Several required orbs may arrive together. Its fill target is fixed when the game starts: later Gate, Door or wall changes don't move it.

Map definitions contain no match state. Snapshots and resync carry per-cell `Boxes` (a Box standing, a pit a Box filled) and the existing per-cell `Durability` for Breakable Walls, Breakable Floors, Sinkholes (1 standing, 0 collapsed), Turrets (the clockwise step they face) and Landfills (a Landfill's remaining fill, from its fill target down to 0), `EdgeDurability` indexed by the map's edges, per-cell `Pressed` state (a Furnace's, Crusher's, Vault's or Turret's holder and working flag, a Vault's shield and a Turret's shot count), and independent `CircuitOn` polarity; the state hash includes the design, requested `PortalMode`, initial output states and these values. Landfills reuse that durability for AI copies and terrain-event replay, with no new wire fields. Polarity cannot be reconstructed from pressed switches because rearming a latch does not reverse it. Runtime topology is isolated between AI search copies; Portal axes are derived from the saved direction settings and current obstructions when topology rebuilds. Animation replays placement events before the first explosion and wave events after transfers. Breakable Barriers and Breakable Floors share the existing wall damage and destruction cues.

Map sounds follow the same displayed steps: the same glass chime when an exploding Link sends an orb across its pair or an orb traverses a Portal pair,
distinct clicks when Latch, Toggle or Hold changes state, a lower note when Toggle or Hold releases, and separate
woody damage and destruction cues for breakable walls and floors, and a hollow falling cue for Holes, Landfills, Sinkholes, Spillways and Crushers. A Landfill that fills plays rising mallet notes as it becomes Floor, and so does a pit a Box falls into, after the falling cue. Sliding Boxes knock softly twice and a launching Spring plays a rising reed. A Furnace's new orb plays the placement note. A Vault raising its shield plays a bright hum sweeping up, C5 G5 E6, a hit on it the breakable wall's damage knock, and breaking it the destruction cue. A Turret firing plays a woody thump and a high ping, C4 then A5, in place of the explosion chord unless Furnaces explode in the same step. Repeated arrivals on an unchanged switch stay silent.
All cues use the orb sounds' C major pentatonic scale, saved mute and volume, shared rate limit and voice cap.
Rate-limited map cues coalesce for the next burst instead of disappearing, including after a chain's final wave.

The game protocol remains version 3 and the map format remains version 2; both identifiers were already introduced after public release v1.0.0, which used protocol 1 and had no custom maps. Breakable Barriers use edge kind 2, Holes terrain 4, Landfills append terrain value 5, Breakable Floors terrain value 6, Sinkholes terrain value 7, and Spillways, Furnaces, Crushers, Boxes, Springs and Vaults component tags 10–15 in format 2. Boxes added the `Boxes` snapshot field with no protocol bump. Sinkhole takes the last value format 2's three terrain bits hold, so any further terrain needs a map format change. Different protocol numbers are rejected before play. Classic rectangular gameplay remains available.

All twelve bots remain legal and deterministic on maps. In the editor, click the map title to rename it and press Enter to finish. On a keyboard, Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes, Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes, and E selects Erase. Editor shortcuts are inactive while renaming or using a dialog. Validation messages appear automatically in the bottom snackbar.

The editor toolbar groups capacity and pressed-switch preview toggles separately from Test and Save. Map size stays in the options menu. Erase highlights the cell or edge that will be removed. Validation outlines faulty Barriers and Gates themselves, using the same edge outline as Erase; floor issues outline their cells.

Hold the middle mouse button on the editor canvas to pan, or hold the right mouse button to erase. Releasing restores the previously selected tool; each right-button erase stroke is undoable. Touch controls continue to use the visible Pan and Erase tools.

Keyboard view controls: press M to toggle Move; press M again to restore the previous tool. Use +/= or numpad + to zoom in, - or numpad - to zoom out, and 0 (also Ctrl/Cmd+0 or numpad 0) to fit and recenter the map. View shortcuts are inactive while typing a title or using a dialog.

Ordinary editor snackbar messages dismiss automatically after five seconds. Validation issues stay visible until resolved.
